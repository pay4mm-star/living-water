Elevator Pit Cleaning: What Building Managers Need to Know

By the Living Water team · 28 years cleaning and restoring GTA properties · Updated November 2026

Short answer: Elevator pits collect garbage, dust, oil and water over time, creating fire, slip and environmental hazards for riders and maintenance crews. Building managers should have pits inspected during routine elevator maintenance and professionally cleaned whenever debris, oil, standing water or odours are present — and immediately after flooding or a sewage backup.

On this page
  1. Why elevator pits get dirty
  2. Warning signs
  3. What professional elevator pit cleaning includes
  4. Why use a specialist?

Why elevator pits get dirty

The pit sits at the lowest point of the hoistway. Debris falls through door gaps, hydraulic and lubricating oil can leak, and groundwater, sprinkler discharge or sewage backups collect there. Because nobody sees it, contamination builds up unnoticed.

Warning signs

  • Garbage or dust accumulation
  • Standing water, mud or sludge
  • Oil sheen or hydraulic fluid on the floor
  • Foul odours from the hoistway
  • Visible cracks or water entry

What professional elevator pit cleaning includes

  1. Coordination with your elevator service provider so the elevator is locked out safely
  2. Removal of debris, sludge and standing water
  3. Oil spill clean-up and proper disposal of hazardous waste
  4. Pressure washing and degreasing of the pit floor, walls and equipment
  5. Sanitizing and deodorizing, especially after sewage backups
  6. Crack repair and pit painting where required

Why use a specialist?

Pit work involves confined spaces, moving equipment and contaminated waste. Elevator companies across Ontario refer pit cleaning to specialists like Living Water so their mechanics can focus on the equipment.

Who is responsible for cleaning the elevator pit?

Usually the building owner or property manager arranges cleaning, coordinated with the elevator maintenance contractor who controls access to the equipment.

How long does elevator pit cleaning take?

Routine cleaning is often completed in a few hours; flood or oil spill clean-up can take longer depending on volume and disposal.
