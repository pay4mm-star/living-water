Is Sandblasting Safe for Brick? Media Blasting Options Explained

By the Living Water team · 28 years cleaning and restoring GTA properties · Updated November 2026

Short answer: Traditional sandblasting with coarse abrasive at high pressure is not safe for most brick — it strips the hard outer face, exposes the soft core to water and freeze-thaw damage, and erodes mortar. Brick can be cleaned or stripped safely with gentler methods: chemical paint strippers, low-pressure micro blasting with fine media, soda blasting, steam, or dry ice blasting, chosen after a test patch.

On this page
  1. Why aggressive sandblasting damages brick
  2. Safer blasting and cleaning options

Why aggressive sandblasting damages brick

Clay brick is fired to create a hard, dense outer skin. Coarse abrasive at high pressure removes that skin along with the dirt or paint, leaving a rough, porous surface that absorbs water. In Ontario's freeze-thaw cycles, absorbed water expands and causes spalling — faces popping off — within a few winters.

Safer blasting and cleaning options

MethodHow it worksBest for
Chemical strippingStripper softens paint, removed with low-pressure rinsingPainted brick, heritage facades
Micro blastingFine media at low pressure, tightly controlledDetailed masonry, stubborn coatings
Soda blastingSodium bicarbonate media, softer than sandDelicate surfaces, soot, light coatings
Dry ice blastingFrozen CO₂ pellets that sublimate on impactCoatings and residue with no media clean-up
Steam cleaningLow-pressure, high-temperature waterHeritage stone and brick, organic staining
Dustless (wet) abrasive blastingAbrasive mixed with water to control dustConcrete, steel, pools, durable masonry

When is sandblasting the right choice?

Abrasive blasting is excellent for steel, aluminium, concrete, pool shells, bridges, tanks and heavy coatings like epoxy. On masonry it should be limited to durable brick or concrete, at low pressure, with fine media — and always after a test panel.

Questions to ask a blasting contractor

  • Will you do a test patch and let me approve it?
  • What media and pressure will you use, and why?
  • How will dust, runoff and neighbouring property be protected?
  • Are you WSIB covered and insured, and can you provide a certificate?

Can painted brick be restored to original?

Usually, yes, with chemical stripping and gentle rinsing or micro blasting. Some paint ghosting may remain on very porous brick.

Is soda blasting safe for brick?

Soda is much softer than sand and is often suitable for brick and stone, but pressure and nozzle distance still matter, and sodium residue must be rinsed well.

Does dustless blasting create dust?

It greatly reduces airborne dust by wetting the media, making it better suited to occupied or urban sites.
