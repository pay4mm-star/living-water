Graffiti Removal Guide: Brick, Concrete, Stone & Metal

By the Living Water team · 28 years cleaning and restoring GTA properties · Updated November 2026

  • Since 199828 years in business
  • 7,500+properties cleaned
  • $5Mliability insured

Short answer: Graffiti is best removed quickly with a remover matched to the paint and the surface — solvent-based removers for spray paint and markers, followed by controlled low-to-medium pressure rinsing — rather than high pressure alone, which drives paint deeper into porous brick and concrete. Anti-graffiti coatings make future tags much easier to remove.

On this page
  1. Why fast removal matters
  2. Approach by surface
  3. Anti-graffiti coatings

Why fast removal matters

Fresh paint sits closer to the surface and comes off more cleanly. The longer it cures, the deeper it penetrates porous materials, and quickly removed tags are less likely to attract more.

Approach by surface

SurfaceTypical approach
BrickRemover dwell, then low-pressure hot rinse; poultice for ghosting
ConcreteRemover plus hot-water pressure rinse
Natural stonepH-appropriate remover, gentle rinse, poultice if needed
Metal & painted surfacesMild solvent that lifts tag but not factory finish
GlassSolvent and blade; etched graffiti needs polishing or film

Anti-graffiti coatings

Sacrificial coatings come off with the graffiti and are re-applied; permanent coatings let tags be wiped or washed off repeatedly. Both should be breathable on masonry.

Will graffiti removal leave a shadow?

Fresh graffiti usually leaves no trace. Old tags on porous brick may leave light ghosting, reduced with poultices or gentle blasting.

Who removes graffiti on private property in Toronto?

Property owners are generally responsible for removing graffiti from their buildings; check your municipality's current graffiti bylaw for timelines.
