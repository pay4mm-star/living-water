How to Clean and Re-Sand Interlock Pavers

By the Living Water team · 28 years cleaning and restoring GTA properties · Updated November 2026

  • $5Mliability insured

Short answer: Interlock is cleaned by pre-treating stains and organic growth, washing with a rotary surface cleaner (not a bare wand) to avoid stripes, flushing out old joint sand and debris, letting the pavers dry completely, then sweeping new polymeric sand into the joints and activating it with a light mist. Sealing is optional and protects colour and helps resist stains.

On this page
  1. Why interlock gets dirty and weedy
  2. Step-by-step professional process
  3. Common mistakes

Why interlock gets dirty and weedy

Joint sand washes out over time, leaving gaps where soil, seeds and moss settle. Oil drips, tire marks, rust from patio furniture and winter salt add stains, and new pavers can bloom with efflorescence.

Step-by-step professional process

  1. Inspect for sunken or broken pavers and drainage problems.
  2. Pre-treat oil, rust and organic stains with suitable cleaners.
  3. Surface-clean with a rotary cleaner for an even, stripe-free finish.
  4. Flush the joints to remove old sand, weeds and moss.
  5. Rinse and dry — polymeric sand needs dry pavers and dry joints.
  6. Re-sand with polymeric sand, compact, and sweep off the surface.
  7. Activate with a fine mist so the sand hardens without hazing.
  8. Seal (optional) once fully cured, if the surface benefits.

Common mistakes

  • Using a narrow wand tip — leaves stripes and erodes the paver face.
  • Applying polymeric sand to damp pavers — causes haze that is hard to remove.
  • Sealing over efflorescence or damp pavers — traps moisture and whitens the sealer.

How often should interlock be re-sanded?

Typically every few years, or whenever joints look empty or weeds start appearing. Polymeric sand lasts longer than regular sand.

Should I seal my interlock?

Sealing is optional. It can enrich colour and make stains easier to clean, but it must be applied to clean, dry pavers with no efflorescence.
