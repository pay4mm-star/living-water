Why winter is elevator pit season

From December to April, GTA buildings track in snow, slush and road salt every day. Meltwater runs through door sills and into the hoistway, salt and grit settle in the pit, and freeze-thaw cycles and spring melt push groundwater through cracks and failing sumps. Left alone, that mix becomes corrosive sludge around buffers, rails and pit equipment — and a slip hazard for the mechanics who have to work there.

  • Salt-laden meltwater corrodes pit steel, buffers and rail brackets
  • Wet debris and garbage become a fire and odour problem
  • Groundwater seepage and sump failures peak during thaws
  • Frozen and burst pipes flood lower levels and pits

How a pit cleaning works

  1. Quote — send the number of elevators, pit photos if you have them, and the building address. Fixed written price.
  2. Schedule — we book a time with you and your elevator contractor, often after hours.
  3. Lock-out — the elevator contractor takes the car out of service and secures it.
  4. Clean — debris and water removed, oil and sludge cleaned up, pit washed, degreased and sanitized.
  5. Report — before-and-after photos and notes on anything we see, such as leaks, cracks or sump issues.

Standards and warranty context in Ontario

Elevating devices in Ontario are regulated by the Technical Standards and Safety Authority (TSSA) under the CSA B44 safety code. TSSA's elevator pre-inspection checklist calls for permanent means to prevent groundwater accumulating in the pit, and for sumps in pits to be covered. For newer condominiums, Tarion's construction performance guidelines state that elevator pits should be clear of standing water or fluids and cleaned of construction debris after construction — useful to document during first-year warranty inspections.

Your elevator contractor remains responsible for the equipment itself; Living Water handles the cleaning, pumping and sanitizing so the pit stays clean, dry and safe to work in.

What affects the price of elevator pit cleaning?

The starting prices above cover a standard pit. Every job is confirmed as a fixed written price. The main factors that can move it are:

  • Number of elevators and pits per building — multi-pit and multi-building programs are priced together
  • Condition — routine debris versus standing water, oil, sludge or sewage
  • Waste disposal — oily or contaminated water and material must be disposed of properly
  • Pit size and depth, and access for pumps and equipment
  • Timing — overnight, weekend or 24/7 emergency response
  • Extras — pit painting, crack repair or deodorizing

Elevator Pit Cleaning FAQ

Anything not covered here? Call (416) 663-8000 and ask.

How much does elevator pit cleaning cost in Toronto?

Living Water's elevator pit cleaning starts at $1,500 for a single pit (up to 4 inches of water), $2,500 for a double pit, $3,500 for a triple pit and $4,500 for four pits. Every price includes full cleaning and sanitizing so the pit is ready for paint. Prices are before HST and we confirm a fixed written quote.

What is included in the elevator pit cleaning price?

Debris and water removal, sludge and salt cleaning, pressure washing and degreasing, and sanitizing, leaving the pit clean and ready for paint. The single-pit price covers up to 4 inches of standing water.

Can you paint the elevator pit after cleaning?

Our cleaning and sanitizing leaves the pit ready for paint, and elevator pit painting and crack repair are available as add-ons.

What if the pit has more than 4 inches of water?

Deeper water, oil, hydraulic fluid or sewage needs extra pumping and disposal, so we quote it separately and tell you before work starts. For active flooding call (647) 833-4343, 24/7.

How often should a condo clean its elevator pits?

At least once a year, and more often in high-traffic buildings. Many property managers book a cleaning after winter to remove salt and meltwater residue, and immediately after any flood, leak or sewage backup.

Who is responsible for cleaning the elevator pit?

The building owner or property manager usually arranges pit cleaning. The elevator contractor maintains the equipment and controls access, so the cleaning is coordinated with them.

Do you work after hours?

Yes. We regularly schedule overnight, early-morning and weekend pit cleaning so residents and tenants are not inconvenienced.

How long is the elevator out of service?

Routine pit cleaning is usually completed in a few hours per elevator. Flood, oil or sewage clean-ups take longer depending on volume and disposal.

Do you provide insurance and WSIB documents?

Yes. We send our Certificate of Insurance ($5M commercial general liability) and WSIB clearance with every quote.

Can you clean pits in multiple buildings?

Yes. We set up one-time, seasonal or recurring programs across property management portfolios in Toronto and the GTA.

What causes water in an elevator pit?

Common causes are groundwater seepage, failed sump pumps, meltwater and salt slush tracked in during winter, sprinkler discharge, burst pipes and floor-washing water entering the hoistway.

How do I choose an elevator pit cleaning company?

Look for a contractor with $5M liability insurance, WSIB clearance, experience coordinating with your elevator company, after-hours availability and a photo report. Living Water provides all of these and a fixed written price.

Do you clean elevator pits in the middle of winter?

Yes. Winter is the busiest season because of salt slush and meltwater, and we work overnight and on weekends so elevators are back in service quickly.
